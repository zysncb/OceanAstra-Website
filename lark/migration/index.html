Lark

The licence is the easy part.

A move to Lark is a data project with a licence attached. This page is what we would tell you in the first meeting: what actually carries across, what does not, and what a sensible sequence looks like.

Overview Pricing and purchasing Migration
What moves

Carries across

  • Mail, with its folder structure and history, mailbox by mailbox
  • Files and shared drives, with their folder tree intact
  • The company directory, departments and reporting lines
  • Calendars, including recurring meetings and room bookings
  • Distribution lists and shared mailboxes, remapped onto groups
  • Documents, converted into Lark Docs where that is better than keeping them as files
What does not

Does not carry across cleanly

Every migration leaves a residue. It is cheaper to hear about it now than to discover it on cutover weekend.

  • Chat history from another platform rarely transfers in a shape anyone can use; we normally keep the old tool read-only for a period instead
  • Deeply nested permission models are usually redesigned rather than copied — copying them carries the problem across too
  • Macro-heavy spreadsheets stay as files. They do not become Base tables by themselves, and pretending otherwise is how a migration slips a month
  • Add-ins written for the old suite need an equivalent found, rebuilt, or dropped on purpose
Sequence

What a sensible plan looks like

  • Inventory — seats, mailbox sizes, file volume, and the integrations nobody remembers until they break
  • Tenant, domain, directory and permissions, proved on a pilot group before anyone else sees it
  • Mail and files migrated in waves, oldest data first, while the old system is still running
  • Cutover, with the old system left read-only rather than switched off
  • Then approvals, Base applications and the AI configuration — once people are in and working, not before
From you

What we need on your side

  • Administrator access to the current tenant, or someone in the room who has it
  • A named person who can decide what the organisation structure should be, because that decision is not ours to make
  • An honest list of the integrations in use, including the ones built by someone who has since left

Send us what you run today.

Headcount, current mail and file platform, rough data volume, and any integration you would be unhappy to lose. We will come back with a sequence and the parts we think are at risk.
