Lark

Lark, supplied and implemented from Dubai.

Lark brings chat, email, documents, approvals, calendar and the company directory into one platform. OceanAstra supplies it under a signed reseller agreement, sets it up, moves your data into it, and makes the AI inside it something your team actually uses.

What Lark is

One platform instead of five

Most companies run the working day across tools that do not know about each other: one for chat, another for mail, a third for files, a fourth for approvals. Each handover between them is a place where work waits, or quietly stops. Lark is the same working day on one platform.

  • Messenger, email, calendar and video meetings
  • Docs, Wiki, Slides and Forms, with the company directory behind them
  • Approval flows, so a decision leaves a record rather than a message
  • Base — tables, dashboards and automation built without code
  • AnyCross and open APIs, for the systems that are not going to move
Why buy through a reseller

What Lark's own checkout cannot do

Lark publishes its prices and will sell you a Pro plan on a credit card in about four minutes. If that is what your company needs, do that — it is faster than talking to us, and we would rather say so now than halfway through a meeting. What the checkout cannot do is the part most companies here get stuck on.

  • A quotation and an invoice in AED, from a company registered in Dubai under trade licence 1646635
  • An invoice raised against your purchase order, through the procurement cycle your finance team already runs
  • Payment by bank transfer, with no company card involved at any point
  • Vendor registration: trade licence, company documents and the forms your finance system needs before a new supplier exists in it
  • One contract covering the licences and the work around them, rather than a subscription on one side and a project on the other
  • People in your time zone for the setup, the migration and the first support call

The AI part

The capability ships with the licence. Using it does not.

Lark has AI in the platform already, which is why so little of it gets used: the capability arrives the day the licence does, and then nothing happens. Deciding which parts of the working day are worth automating, and configuring them so people reach for them without being reminded, is a separate job that no licence performs. That job is the one we take on.

  • AI meeting notes and searchable minutes, so what a meeting decided stays findable
  • An AI teammate inside the workspace, set against your own documents, approvals and company knowledge rather than the open internet
  • Automation in Base, so the recurring judgement calls in a process are carried by the system rather than by whoever remembers them
  • Real-time translation in chat and documents, so a team that does not share one working language still works in one channel
  • AnyCross, so the automation reaches the ERP and the finance system instead of stopping at the chat window
  • A short list, agreed with you, of what is worth automating first — and what is not worth automating at all
